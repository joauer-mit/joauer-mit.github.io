Works in progress

  • How Far Can Firms Push Product Claims? Demand, Attention, and Strategic Representation

    Presented at MIT Research Seminar in Marketing.

    A structural model of firms’ product claims and rationally inattentive consumer demand, studying when attention and competition discipline exaggeration.

  • Optimal Bandwagons: Consensus Building with Public Pressure

    Presented at MIT Behavioural Lunch.

    A model of evidence access and voting order showing when public pressure helps secure unanimous approval and when an approval-seeking sponsor benefits from protecting independent scrutiny.

  • AI Assistance and Misperceived Learning (with Eric Gao)

    An experiment testing whether AI assistance distorts beliefs about human capital accumulation by inducing overprediction of later unaided performance.

  • Commitment Cascades (with Eric Gao)

    A model of delayed commitment demand in which naive present-biased agents update from failed intentions, generating cross-environment cascades in sophistication and take-up.

  • AI and Skill Development in Frontline Health Work (with Madhavi Jha and Lauren Rice)

    A planned experiment testing how AI advice and feedback during training affect community health workers’ later unaided counselling skills and transfer to unfamiliar cases.

  • Diagnosing Behavioural Hazard in the Age of Algorithms (with Zachary Wojtowicz)

    A planned field experiment testing whether AI support tailored to patients’ barriers improves diabetic retinopathy follow-up beyond standard information and reminders.

  • Modern Slavery (with Ian Jewitt)

    A decision-theoretic model of risky migration and trafficking vulnerability under uncertainty.
